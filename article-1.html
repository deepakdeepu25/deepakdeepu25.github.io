AI Is Changing How We Search — So What Happens to Websites?

AI is changing how people find information. If an AI can answer a question without sending someone to a website, what happens to the websites that built the internet?

Published August 27, 2026 · EEE — Evilelite Enterprises

For more than two decades, the basic idea of the internet was simple.

You had a question.

You opened Google.

You searched.

You clicked a website.

You read the answer.

That model built an enormous online ecosystem of publishers, bloggers, businesses, affiliate websites and independent creators.

But something fundamental is changing.

People can now ask ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and other AI systems a question and receive a synthesized answer without opening several websites.

Google itself is also moving toward AI-assisted search.

If AI can answer the question, what happens to the websites that used to provide the answer?

The search experience is changing

Traditional search was essentially a directory.

A search engine would provide a list of pages and the user would decide which result was useful.

AI search changes the first interaction.

Instead of simply receiving a list of links, users can receive a generated explanation that combines information from multiple sources.

That is incredibly convenient.

For simple questions, a person may no longer need to visit a website at all.

For example:

“What is compound interest?”

An AI assistant can explain it in seconds. There may be little reason for someone to open several finance websites just to understand the basic definition.

Are AI answers actually reducing website traffic?

There is evidence pointing in both directions.

Search engines are increasingly integrating AI-generated answers into the search experience. These systems can still provide links to sources and encourage users to explore topics in greater depth.

At the same time, independent research has found that AI-generated answers can reduce the number of users who click through to traditional organic results.

That creates a real challenge for publishers.

The important point:

AI search is not necessarily killing the entire web. But it is changing which types of web content are valuable.

The biggest problem is generic information

Imagine two websites.

Website A

“What is artificial intelligence?”

It provides a 1,000-word article explaining the definition of AI.

An AI assistant can answer this question immediately.

Website B

“We tested 25 AI video generators using the same five prompts. Here are the results.”

Now things are different.

Website B contains original testing, observations, data, screenshots, comparisons and specific results.

An AI can summarize the information, but the underlying research still has value.

The internet may move from information to evidence

For years, many websites competed to explain the same information.

The future may reward websites that provide something harder to reproduce:

Evidence.

Original research.
Product testing.
Experiments.
Reviews.
Comparisons.
Databases.
Calculators.
Case studies.
First-hand experience.

In other words:

Don't just tell people something. Show them why they should believe it.

What happens to affiliate websites?

Affiliate websites are particularly interesting.

A basic article such as “What is the best laptop?” can easily become generic.

But imagine an article called:

“We tested five laptops under ₹70,000 for trading and productivity.”

Now the reader has a reason to visit.

The website can provide real testing, performance measurements, screenshots, pros and cons, comparison tables and purchase information.

That creates a much stronger business model.

What does this mean for bloggers?

The old strategy was often:

Find keyword → write article → rank → receive traffic.

That strategy is becoming less reliable.

A stronger strategy is:

Find a real problem → research it → add original value → publish useful evidence → build authority → give readers a reason to visit.

Search engines still matter.

But search engines are no longer the only discovery layer.

AI assistants are becoming another way information is discovered.

Does this mean websites are dead?

No.

It means low-value websites are under more pressure.

People still need websites when they want detailed research, product comparisons, reviews, original data, tools, interactive resources, communities and first-hand experiences.

AI can give you an answer.

But sometimes you want to know:

“How did they reach that answer?”

That's where the web becomes valuable again.

The future isn't Google vs AI

It may not be a question of whether Google or AI wins.

The more interesting possibility is that the internet becomes a combination of both.

People may discover an idea through an AI assistant. They may then visit the original source, watch a video, compare products, read a detailed review or use a tool.

The websites that survive this transition won't necessarily be the websites with the most articles.

They may be the websites with the most useful reasons to exist.
