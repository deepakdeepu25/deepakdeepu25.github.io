Passkeys in 2026: Are Passwords Finally Dying?

Passwords have protected online accounts for decades. Passkeys are now offering a different approach to authentication. Here's what they are, how they work and whether you should use them.

September 2026 · EEE — Evilelite Enterprises

Think about how many passwords you have used during your life.

Email accounts. Social media. Banking. Shopping. Work applications. Cloud storage. Streaming services.

Every account traditionally required another secret that you had to remember, protect and occasionally reset.

The problem is that passwords are difficult for humans to manage securely.

That is why the technology industry has been working toward a different approach: passkeys.

Passkeys replace the traditional password with a cryptographic credential that can be unlocked using the security of your device.

What Is a Passkey?

A passkey is a passwordless sign-in credential based on public-key cryptography and the FIDO authentication standards.

When you create a passkey, your device generates a cryptographic key pair.

The private key remains protected by the credential system on your device or passkey provider, while the service receives the corresponding public key.

When you sign in later, the service can verify that you possess the correct credential without asking you to type a traditional password.

Why Do Passkeys Exist?

Passwords have several fundamental weaknesses.

  • People reuse passwords across multiple accounts.
  • Passwords can be stolen through phishing.
  • Password databases can be compromised.
  • Users forget passwords and need recovery processes.
  • Password reset systems can become another attack surface.

Adding multi-factor authentication can improve security, but many common second factors can still be targeted by phishing.

Passkeys were designed to address these problems at the authentication level rather than simply adding another layer on top of passwords.

Passkeys vs Passwords

Passwords Passkeys
User creates and remembers a secret Cryptographic credential is created by the device
Can be typed into a fake website Designed to resist phishing
Often reused Credential is tied to the specific service
Frequently requires password resets No traditional password to remember

How Does a Passkey Work?

The process sounds complicated, but using one is usually simple.

Step 1 — Create the passkey

A website or application asks whether you want to create a passkey. Your device then generates the necessary cryptographic credentials.

Step 2 — Verify yourself

Depending on your device and configuration, you may use a fingerprint, face recognition, device PIN or another supported method.

Step 3 — Sign in later

When you return to the website, your device can use the passkey to authenticate you without requiring you to type a password.

Why Passkeys Are Harder to Phish

This is probably the most important security advantage.

A traditional password is something a user can accidentally type into a fake website.

A properly implemented passkey does not work that way. The cryptographic credential is associated with the legitimate service and authentication process rather than being a secret that the user manually types into any webpage.

The UK's National Cyber Security Centre assessed FIDO2 credentials, including passkeys, as at least as secure as traditional MFA against common credential attacks and said it plans to recommend passkeys where services support them.

The biggest change isn't that passkeys are easier to remember. It's that there is nothing for you to remember in the first place.

What Happens If You Lose Your Phone?

This is one of the most common questions about passkeys.

The answer depends on how the passkey is managed.

Many passkey providers synchronize passkeys between a user's devices using protected systems. This can make moving to a new device much easier than recovering dozens of forgotten passwords.

Hardware security keys can also be used for users who want a device-based credential that is separate from their normal phone.

However, account recovery still matters. A good authentication system needs a secure recovery process for situations such as device loss, account takeover or changes between ecosystems.

Are Passkeys Completely Safe?

No security technology should be described as completely safe.

Passkeys significantly reduce important classes of credential attacks, but the surrounding account and recovery system still matters.

Attackers can still target users through malware, stolen devices, compromised accounts, social engineering and other techniques.

Passkeys reduce the risk of password-based attacks.

They do not make every part of your digital life automatically secure.

Are Passkeys Actually Becoming Mainstream?

The answer in 2026 appears to be yes.

The FIDO Alliance's 2026 global research estimates that approximately 5 billion passkeys are now in active use worldwide.

Its research also found that 90% of surveyed consumers were familiar with passkeys and 75% had enabled a passkey on at least one account.

Among organizations surveyed, 68% were deploying, piloting or rolling out passkeys for employee authentication.

That does not mean passwords are disappearing tomorrow.

Many services still support passwords, and organizations continue to deal with legacy systems, recovery requirements and user adoption.

Why Passwords Aren't Dead Yet

Passkeys are growing rapidly, but replacing passwords across the entire internet is a massive transition.

Some websites do not support passkeys yet.

Some users are unfamiliar with the technology.

Organizations also have older applications and authentication systems that cannot simply be replaced overnight.

The likely future is therefore not an instant switch.

Instead, passwords and passkeys will coexist while more services gradually make passkeys the preferred option.

Should You Start Using Passkeys?

If an important service you use offers a passkey, there is a strong reason to consider enabling it.

Start with your most important accounts.

  • Primary email
  • Major cloud accounts
  • Financial services that support passkeys
  • Important work accounts
  • Developer and technology accounts

Keep your devices protected, maintain appropriate recovery options, and make sure you understand how your chosen passkey provider handles synchronization and account recovery.

The Bottom Line

Passwords have been around for a very long time because they are simple.

But simplicity for the website does not always mean simplicity or security for the user.

Passkeys represent a fundamental change in authentication: instead of asking users to remember increasingly complicated secrets, the technology can use cryptographic credentials protected by the devices people already use.

They aren't perfect, and passwords aren't disappearing overnight.

But with billions of passkeys now in use and major technology organizations supporting the standard, this is no longer an experimental idea sitting on the sidelines.

Passwords may not disappear tomorrow. But the internet is clearly building a future where you need fewer of them.
